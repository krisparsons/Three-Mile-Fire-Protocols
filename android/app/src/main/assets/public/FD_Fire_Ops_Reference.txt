SECTION 1: FIREFIGHTER I (FF1) CORE OPERATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 PPE & SCBA RAPID DONNING PROCEDURE (NFPA 1001 Skill Sheet 1-I)
Target Time: Complete donning of turnout gear and SCBA within 60 seconds.

Rapid Donning Checklist:
  Step 1: Turnout boots pulled up fully, suspenders positioned securely over shoulders.
  Step 2: Turnout coat donned, storm collar secured, zipper/clips locked completely.
  Step 3: Flash hood put on and pulled down around neck.
  Step 4: SCBA harness placed over shoulders (backpack style or over-the-head method).
  Step 5: Waist belt and chest straps tightened firmly, hoses clear of twists.
  Step 6: Main cylinder valve fully opened; Heads-Up Display (HUD) checked; low-air alarm verifies during pressurization.
  Step 7: Facepiece donned, straps tightened evenly from bottom (neck) to top (crown).
  Step 8: Check seal by covering intake with hand and inhaling deeply (mask should collapse slightly against face).
  Step 9: Flash hood pulled up to cover head with NO skin exposed anywhere around facepiece or neck.
  Step 10: Helmet donned, chin strap tightened. Turnout gloves donned and cuffs secured under sleeves.

Critical Safety Checkpoints:
  * Ensure ZERO skin is visible between the facepiece seal, flash hood, and turnout coat collar.
  * SCBA low-air alarm (bell or whistle) must actuate briefly during initial pressurization to verify operation.
  * Bypass valve must be fully closed and purge button verified before entering IDLH atmospheres.
  * Never rush seal testing: mask seal failure inside a structure fire causes immediate carbon monoxide and hydrogen cyanide inhalation.

Immediately Dangerous to Life or Health (IDLH) Limits:
  * Carbon Monoxide (CO): > 1,200 ppm
  * Oxygen Concentration: < 19.5%
  * Presence of visible smoke, toxic combustion products, or oxygen-deficient environment requires 100% SCBA utilization.


1.2 FIRE DYNAMICS & EXTREME PHENOMENA
Understanding Compartment Fire Behavior & Thermal Layering:

01 / Rollover (Flame Licking):
  * Definition: Unburned pyrolysis products accumulate near the ceiling and mix with oxygen until igniting. Licks of flame roll across the smoke layer.
  * Warning: Imminent Flashover indicator! Cool the upper thermal column immediately.

02 / Flashover:
  * Definition: Simultaneous ignition of all combustible surfaces in a compartment.
  * Conditions: Compartment temperature reaches approximately 1,100°F (593°C). Radiant heat warms all fuels to ignition temperature simultaneously.
  * Survival: Zero survival rate for unprotected occupants or firefighters trapped inside.
  * Action: Stay low immediately and apply direct stream to upper smoke layer.

03 / Backdraft:
  * Definition: Explosive ignition of superheated, oxygen-depleted flammable gases when fresh air is rapidly introduced into a confined compartment.
  * Indicators: Puffy yellow/brown smoke, smoke pushing through small cracks under pressure, window glass stained with dark soot and hot to touch, absence of active flame.
  * Action: Perform vertical ventilation at highest point prior to entry to release superheated gases.

Thermal Layering & Nozzle Discipline:
  * Superheated gases rise, forming hot upper layers and cooler lower air.
  * Water droplets directed into the upper column expand to steam at a 1,700:1 ratio.
  * Excessive water steam generation destroys thermal balance, pushing scalding steam down onto firefighters and victims.
  * Technique: Apply short, pulsing direct bursts to cool the overhead smoke layer without generating excess steam.


1.3 LADDERS, CARRIES, AND SEARCH PROCEDURES

Ground Ladder Positioning Rules:
  * Standard Climbing Angle: 75 degrees (distance from wall = 1/4 of ladder working length).
  * Roof Access: Extend 3 to 5 rungs above the roofline to provide a visible, secure handhold.
  * Window Rescue: Place ladder tip just below the window sill level.
  * Window Ventilation: Place ladder tip even with the top of the window frame on the windward (upwind) side.

Angle Formula:
  * Divide height to be reached by 4. Place the ladder butt that exact distance away from the building wall.
  * Fly Placement: Fly section faces OUTWARD on wooden extension ladders; fly section faces INWARD on modern metal or fiberglass ladders (per NFPA standard recommendations).

Primary Search Operations:
  * Conduct in systematic patterns (left-hand or right-hand wall search).
  * Maintain physical/visual contact with walls, thermal imaging cameras (TIC), or tool vectors.
  * Keep low under the smoke layer to preserve thermal insulation and visibility.

VEIS Protocol (Vent, Enter, Isolate, Search):
  * V - VENT: Break/remove window to create entry point.
  * E - ENTER: Climb into room through window.
  * I - ISOLATE: IMMEDIATELY close the interior door to isolate the search room from the advancing fire path and flow path.
  * S - SEARCH: Rapidly search the room for victims, then exit back through the window.


1.4 TACTICAL VENTILATION & HOSE STREAMS

Water Streams:
  * Solid Stream: Maximum penetration, reach, minimum steam production, maintains thermal layering.
  * Fog Stream: Maximum surface heat absorption, shorter reach, susceptible to wind and thermal disruption.

Hose Line Operations:
  * Stand on the inside of backup line curves to absorb nozzle reaction forces.
  * Maintain backup firefighter position 3 to 4 feet behind the nozzle operator.

Hydraulic Ventilation:
  * Technique: Position nozzle 2 feet back from window/door opening. Set nozzle to a wide fog pattern covering 85% of the opening area. Direct spray outward to create a low-pressure draft pulling smoke/gases out of the structure.

SECTION 2: FIREFIGHTER II (FF2) ADVANCED OPERATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 ADVANCED FIRE SUPPRESSION & FOAM OPERATIONS (NFPA 1001 Section 5.3)

Foam Application Methods:
  1. Roll-On Method: Direct foam stream onto ground in front of flammable liquid spill, allowing foam to roll gently across liquid surface.
  2. Bank-Down Method: Bounce foam stream off an object (wall, tank side, vehicle) so foam runs down and blankets the fuel.
  3. Rain-Down Method: Shoot foam stream high into air above spill, allowing foam to rain gently over the fuel surface without disrupting liquid.

Class A vs Class B Foams:
  * Class A Foam (Structural / Wildland): Reduces water surface tension for faster soaking into wood/paper. Proportioning: 0.1% to 1.0%.
  * Class B Foam (Flammable Liquids): Creates aqueous film blanket suppressing vapors and excluding oxygen.
    - Hydrocarbons (Gasoline, Diesel, Oil): Proportion at 3%.
    - Polar Solvents (Ethanol, Acetone, Alcohol): Require AR-AFFF proportioned at 6%.


2.2 INCIDENT COMMAND SYSTEM (ICS) ON SCENE

Tactical On-Scene Size-Up (4-Part Radio Brief):
  1. Unit designation & arrival status (e.g., "Engine 1 on scene").
  2. Brief incident description (e.g., "Two-story residential structure, heavy smoke showing from floor 2").
  3. Operational strategy declaration ("Offensive interior attack").
  4. Command establishment & post location ("Engine 1 establishing Main Street Command").

Span of Control:
  * NIMS Standard: 3 to 7 subordinates per supervisor (Optimum ratio = 5).
  * Transfer of Command: Must be conducted FACE-TO-FACE whenever possible, with a detailed brief on incident progress, crew positions, safety hazards, and resource allocations.


2.3 VEHICLE RESCUE & EXTRICATION TECHNIQUES

Glass Management:
  * Laminated Glass (Windshields & modern side glass): High strength, plastic interlayer. Requires windshield saw, reciprocating saw, or ax. Does NOT shatter easily.
  * Tempered Glass (Side & rear windows): Shatters into small harmless pebbles. Use spring-loaded center punch in bottom corner of glass.

Electric Vehicle (EV) & High-Voltage Safety:
  * High-voltage wiring is color-coded ORANGE (up to 650V DC). NEVER cut orange wiring!
  * Shut off ignition key, remove key fob 20+ feet away, pull high-voltage manual disconnect service plug if visible.
  * Supplemental Restraint Systems (SRS / Airbags): Capacitors retain stored power for 10-15 minutes after battery disconnect.
  * Follow 5-10-20 Rule: Maintain 5 inches clearance from side airbags, 10 inches from steering wheel, 20 inches from passenger dashboard airbag.


2.4 PRESERVATION OF EVIDENCE & FIRE ORIGIN

Fire Scene Protection Rules:
  * Minimize structural disruption in suspected area of fire origin.
  * Post a sentry or place red fire line tape around the perimeter.
  * Avoid washing away potential accelerant evidence with heavy hose streams.
  * Do NOT overhaul, remove ashes, charcoal, or furniture from origin room until fire investigator inspects and documents scene.

Indications of Intentional / Arson Fires:
  * Multiple independent points of origin.
  * Pour patterns ("V-patterns" or burn trails on floors showing liquid accelerants).
  * Missing personal valuables or family heirlooms prior to fire.
  * Incendiary devices (bottles, timers, matches, unusual wiring).

SECTION 3: FIRE HYDRAULICS FORMULAS & PUMP OPERATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 PUMP DISCHARGE PRESSURE (PDP) FORMULA
  PDP = NP + FL + EL + APP

Where:
  * PDP = Pump Discharge Pressure (PSI)
  * NP  = Nozzle Pressure (PSI)
  * FL  = Friction Loss (PSI)
  * EL  = Elevation Loss/Gain (PSI)
  * APP = Appliance Loss (PSI)


3.2 FRICTION LOSS (FL) FORMULA
  FL = C * (Q / 100)^2 * (L / 100)

Where:
  * C = Hose Diameter Friction Loss Coefficient
  * Q = Flow Rate in Gallons Per Minute (GPM)
  * L = Hose Lay Length in Feet


3.3 HOSE DIAMETER COEFFICIENTS (C):
  * 1.0" Booster Hose:   C = 150
  * 1.5" Attack Hose:    C = 24
  * 1.75" Attack Hose:   C = 15.5
  * 2.0" Attack Hose:    C = 8
  * 2.5" Supply/Line:    C = 2
  * 3.0" Supply Line:    C = 0.8
  * 5.0" Large Diameter: C = 0.08


3.4 NOZZLE PRESSURE (NP) PRESETS:
  * Standard Fog Nozzle:     100 PSI
  * Low-Pressure Fog Nozzle: 75 PSI
  * Smooth Bore Handline:    50 PSI
  * Smooth Bore Master Stream: 80 PSI


3.5 ELEVATION FACTOR (EL):
  * Formula: EL = Elevation in Feet * 0.434
  * Rule of Thumb: Add 5 PSI per floor (above ground) or subtract 5 PSI per floor (below ground).


3.6 APPLIANCE LOSS (APP) PRESETS:
  * Wye / Siamese / Manifold (flow > 350 GPM): +5 PSI
  * Master Stream Appliance:                   +10 PSI
  * Aerial Waterway / Riser:                  +15 PSI

SECTION 4: FIREFIGHTER I & II Q&A STUDY GUIDE (FLASHCARDS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1 [FF1 - Fire Behavior]:
What are the four components of the Fire Tetrahedron?
A1: Oxygen, Fuel, Heat, and a Self-Sustaining Chemical Chain Reaction.

Q2 [FF1 - PPE & SCBA]:
How often must SCBA cylinders be hydrostatically tested?
A2: Composite cylinders must be hydrostatically tested every 5 years (15-year maximum lifespan); Steel and Aluminum cylinders every 5 years (no set expiration if they pass).

Q3 [FF1 - Ladders]:
What is the standard angle and climbing angle for a fire service ground extension ladder?
A3: 75 degrees. The fly section should face outward on wooden extension ladders, but fly section faces INWARD on modern metal or fiberglass ladders.

Q4 [FF1 - Hose & Streams]:
What is water hammer and how do you prevent it?
A4: Water hammer is a pressure surge caused when water in motion is forced to stop suddenly (e.g., closing a nozzle/valve too fast). It can damage hoses, pumps, and water mains. Prevent it by opening and closing all valves and nozzles SLOWLY.

Q5 [FF1 - Ventilation]:
What is the difference between horizontal and vertical ventilation?
A5: Horizontal ventilation uses doors and windows to clear heat/smoke. Vertical ventilation creates openings in the roof or highest ceiling point to allow heat and gases to escape upward.

Q6 [FF2 - ICS / NIMS]:
What are the five major functional areas of the Incident Command System (ICS)?
A6: Command, Operations, Planning, Logistics, and Finance/Administration.

Q7 [FF2 - Extrication]:
In vehicle extrication, what is the proper sequence of securing the vehicle and hazards?
A7: 1. Establish scene safety and traffic control.
    2. Control hazards (fire, power lines, fuel leaks).
    3. Stabilize the vehicle (chock wheels, cribbing).
    4. Turn off ignition, move key fob, disconnect batteries (negative terminal first).

Q8 [FF2 - Foam Operations]:
What is the expansion ratio of medium expansion foam?
A8: 20:1 to 200:1. (Low expansion foam is up to 20:1; High expansion foam is 200:1 to 1000:1).

Q9 [FF2 - Pre-Incident Planning]:
What is the main objective of conducting a pre-incident survey/plan?
A9: To gather critical site-specific safety information (structural type, hazardous materials, water supply, utility shutoffs) to assist incident commanders in tactical decision-making and safety.

Q10 [FF2 - Evidence Preservation]:
What is a firefighter's primary responsibility at a fire scene regarding cause determination?
A10: Observe unusual circumstances during response/arrival, preserve physical evidence of fire origin, and protect the chain of custody by limiting access to the area.
